## Questions

### 1. Privacy versus security (8 points)

Define privacy and security in one sentence each. Give one example of a system that is technically secure but still violates privacy.

### 2. Lifecycle control map (12 points)

### 3. Data states (10 points)

Classify each item as data **at rest**, **in motion**, or **in use**, then give its most relevant control:

- an encrypted SQL backup;
- a SIS-to-portal API request;
- student rows loaded into a Jupyter notebook;
- a dashboard query result waiting in memory.

Why is encryption at rest alone insufficient?

### 4. Prevent, detect, respond (10 points)

A staff account exports an unusually large volume of restricted records at 02:00. Give one preventive, two detective, and two response controls. Name the evidence needed to reconstruct the event.

### 5. Re-identification (12 points)

A public school-health report removes names and student IDs but includes exact age, postcode, rare condition, year level, and school. Explain why this may still identify a student. Propose three changes that preserve useful insight while reducing disclosure risk.

### 6. Classify, control, retain (12 points)

Classify the following as **public**, **internal**, **confidential**, or **restricted**, and justify a proportionate access/retention decision for each:

- public school calendar;
- staff operational memo;
- parent contact and fee records;
- student medical and behavioural notes.

### 7. GDPR and Australian APPs (10 points)

Correct all three claims:

1. "GDPR applies to every Australian organisation."
2. "Consent is always the required lawful basis under GDPR."
3. "Australian privacy law gives everyone the same unconditional right to deletion as GDPR."

### 8. Ownership and auditability (10 points)

Assign an accountable role to purpose/access/retention approval, secure pipeline implementation, model use, incident monitoring, legal interpretation, and business reporting. List four artifacts that demonstrate governance actually happened.

### 9. Cambridge Analytica (8 points)

Why is the Facebook/Cambridge Analytica case a privacy and governance failure even if it is not described as a conventional system intrusion? Give three controls that address the root cause.

### 10. Assessment 3 live clarification (8 points)

In four concise bullets, state the lecturer's guidance on:

- weekly versus cumulative counts;
- graph relationships versus geographic adjacency;
- presentation audience and emphasis;
- notebook execution state.
