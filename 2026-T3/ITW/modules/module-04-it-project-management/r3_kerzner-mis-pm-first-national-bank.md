## [pp. 72-73] Setting
- **First National Bank (FNB)** is a fast-growing Midwest (Ohio) bank that has expanded by buying smaller banks. Over five years its staff grew 35% and its assets grew 70%.
- **Information Services:** the systems and programming staff grew from 12 to more than 75 and is expected to grow by another 50%.
- **History of misalignment:** IS used to report to Consumer Banking and Operations. As a result, early systems sped up consumer transactions and largely ignored the information needs of the rest of the bank, which caused high-level conflict over who gets IS resources.
- **Fix:** a separate **Information Services Division (ISD)** was created, reporting to the president (Exhibit I).
  - Its units are Systems Research, Retail Systems, Corporate Systems and Operations.

## [pp. 73-74] The Priorities Committee
- **Members:** the heads of each major operating division (Exhibit II marks them "P"), including ISD.
- **Purpose:** to make sure staff and hardware go only to systems that can be **cost-justified**.
- **Cadence:** it meets monthly, reaffirms existing rankings and ranks new requests.
- **Bank policy:** the *only* route to IS development funding is a request that the committee approves and ranks.
- **Key document:** the **project proposal**.

## [pp. 74-77] The project proposal life cycle (Exhibit III)
```
User problem statement -> ISD Systems Research impact statement -> Priorities Committee? --no--> end
                                                                        | yes
                                                  full project proposal -> Committee? --no--> end
                                                                        | yes
                                                  ranked priority + milestone schedule -> to ISD
```
- **Impact statement** (from ISD's perspective) covers:
  - feasibility and complexity
  - fit with long-range ISD plans
  - resource commitment
  - similar past requests
  - unique problems
  - a broad cost estimate
- **Full proposal** contents:
  - a detailed problem statement
  - alternative solutions
  - impact on the user division, ISD and other divisions
  - cost estimates
  - an approximate schedule
  - cost-benefit analysis
  - long-range implications
  - a recommended course of action
- **Who argues for it:** the user sponsor must attend the committee to argue for approval and priority. The outcome is drop, table, or rank against ongoing work.
- **Design intent:** priority is set by a cross-section of executives, not by data processing. This avoids sponsors blaming IS when their project ranks low.
- **Cost of proposals:** Systems Research time spent on proposals is ISD overhead and is not charged to projects (p. 77).

## [pp. 77-78] Project life cycle and estimating
- **Three phases:**
  1. Systems definition: project plan, user requirements, systems definition, advisability study.
  2. Design and development: preliminary design, subsystems design, program design, programming and testing.
  3. Implementation: implementation, system test, production control turnover, user training, acceptance.
- **Task list:** the PM system lists **400+ standard tasks**. The PM tailors them, then estimates hours per task.
- **"Moving window" estimating:** detailed estimates only for the next subphase and summary estimates beyond it. There are re-estimates at five points, because the effort is unknowable at the start (p. 78).

## [pp. 78-79] Funding
- **The project plan** is the official funding document. It needs a task network, a milestone schedule, a cost estimate and a budget request, and the sponsor and the systems general manager sign it off before user requirements start.
- **Refunding points:**
  - after the advisability study (1d)
  - after preliminary design (2a)
  - after program design (2c)
  - after implementation (3a)
  - Each point requires a revised plan and an explanation of variances.
- **Design freezes:** after a freeze, a change needs a re-estimate and sponsor approval (p. 79).

## [pp. 79-80] System quality reviews
- **Ten quality reviews**, one at the end of each subphase. Seven are joint user and DP reviews; three are DP-only technical reviews.
- **Side benefit:** users are forced to get involved in, and contribute to, the design.
- A **Quality Review Board** of 2-8 people, appointed by the PM with sponsor and GM approval, reviews each subphase's deliverables. It either records "concurrence" or lists the changes needed before the next subphase starts. Board time is charged to the project.

## [pp. 80-81] Team staffing
- **Appointment:** once a project tops the list, the development manager appoints a PM from the analysts. The PM's planning time is charged to the project.
- **Getting people:** the PM has to **negotiate** for analysts (with development) and programmers (with support), and report resource-caused delays to the GM.
- **Rates:** staff are charged at $76/hr and computer time at $164/hr.
- 🔴 **No user personnel on the team:** all members come from ISD (p. 81).

## [pp. 81-84] The case: the Corporate Database (CDB)
- **Problem:**
  - Most profit came from corporate customers, yet IS effort went to small consumer accounts.
  - No one knew which services a corporate client used, or how profitable that client was.
  - Contact officers called on clients ad hoc.
- **Sponsor:** John Hart, a senior Corporate Banking veteran close to retirement, made executive VP of a new Corporate Marketing Division. He stayed on until "his" system was running.
- **Proposal:**
  - Systems Research wrote it very fast, almost entirely from Hart's input. Other divisions were "too busy" and deferred to him.
  - It recommended interfaces from existing systems, bringing time-sharing in-house, temporary capture of manual data, consolidating manual systems (possibly reorganising departments) and Corporate Marketing control of all CDB analysis.
  - It was ranked the **highest-priority MIS project** in the bank.
- **Execution:**
  - **PM:** Jim Gunn, the most experienced PM available, though from the Financial Division. He had open questions on scope and on how CDB users related to each other.
  - **Rushed planning:** Hart dismissed the meetings as a waste of time, and the GM pushed for speed, so Gunn rushed a general plan that Hart signed.
  - **Under-resourced:** he asked for 4 analysts and got 2 juniors plus 1 more later, with no seniors. His warning to the GM got no reply, so he took on a heavy load himself.
  - **User resistance:** user meetings were cancelled, and Consumer and Corporate Banking became hostile. They feared losing functions to Corporate Marketing.
  - **Sponsor pressure:** Hart was unhappy about overruns and kept ordering people to attend.
  - **Silence:** Gunn knew the quality was poor but did not escalate again, partly because his own appraisal was coming up.
- **Outcome:**
  - At the user-requirements review, **over 90% of tasks had to be redone**, and Gunn was removed.
  - A new PM and three seniors were added and more funding was given. Requirements were redone over the users' protests.
  - Within three months the new PM resigned, Hart retired and **the CDB was tabled** (p. 84).

## [p. 84] Synopsis (author's framing)
- **Structure:** all FNB projects run through ISD with assigned PMs. Some people think it is a matrix, but it is not.
- **The task:** explain **why the CDB project failed**.

## [pp. 84-85] Discussion questions (paraphrased, 17 in source)
| Theme | Questions ask about |
|---|---|
| Diagnosis | FNB's strengths, its weaknesses and the main problem (with a defended answer) |
| Structure | How many people the PM reported to; whether the PM stayed inside the vertical structure; who was on the team; whether a PM who previously worked alongside team members is a problem |
| Resistance | Resistance from management; resistance to change; duplicated work |
| Process | How fast communication was; how much paperwork there was; whether a PM methodology existed and would have helped |
| Strategy | Recommendations; whether FNB did strategic planning for PM or rushed in; why organisations skip strategic planning or benchmarking |

## Why it matters for ITW601 (my notes, not the source)
1. **A proposal is a governance document, not a sales pitch.** FNB has a strong formal process: committee, impact statement, quality boards and refunding gates. The project still failed on people: one sponsor's voice, no users on the team, juniors only, and a PM who stopped escalating.
2. **Single-source requirements are a risk.** "That project is John's baby" (p. 82) is the warning sign. For my placement it maps to a project shaped by one requester: validate with every affected group before scoping.
3. **Escalate early, in writing.** Gunn's silence before his appraisal is the ethics link back to Module 3.
4. **A1/A2 link:** the problem statement, alternatives, impact, cost-benefit and recommendation give a ready skeleton for the A2 project synopsis.
