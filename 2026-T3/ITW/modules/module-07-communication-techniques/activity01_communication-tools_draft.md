**1. Which tools?**

In my placement the tools sort into three groups by what they are for:

- **Deciding:** a weekly checkpoint with the academic stakeholder, and a written rules note she confirms before any rule is applied. Decisions go into a control journal with a date and a reason.
- **Tracking:** a ticket per request (helpdesk or issue tracker), a Kanban board, and pull-request review so every change has a reviewer and a record.
- **Showing:** a one-page meeting pack, a worked example on a real case, and a workbook the stakeholder can open. These do more work than any document, because the stakeholder can point at a row and say "that one is wrong".

Email and calls sit across all three.

**2. How does understanding them help in hard situations?**

Cerri (2000) puts three preparation steps before the message is sent: know the setting, match the listener, understand their frame. The tool choice is step 1. A rule dispute goes in a worked example, not an email thread. A scope question goes on the board where the sponsor can see the cost. A misunderstanding gets a call, because text hides the cue that step 5 (check it landed) depends on.

The hardest moment so far was a stakeholder reply that reopened a result I had treated as final. The fix was not a better email; it was asking, on a call, what she meant by "like the Year 12 one". Ferguson (1977) would say the picture in her head and the one in mine were different, and only a shared artefact shows that.

**Question for you:** which tool do you reach for when a stakeholder's words and their meaning seem to differ?
