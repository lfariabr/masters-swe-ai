## Key Highlights

### 1. Northcutt, S. (2004). IT ethics handbook: Introduction.

**Citation:** Northcutt, S. (2004). *IT ethics handbook: Right and wrong for IT professionals*. Syngress, introductory chapter, pp. 1-14.

**Purpose:** Sets a shared vocabulary of ethics, morals, policy, law and culture before the book's case discussions. Local copy: [r1_it-ethics-handbook-introduction.pdf](r1_it-ethics-handbook-introduction.pdf).

---

#### 1. Starting claims
- **No new ethics for IT:** "stealing is still stealing". The internet adds a sense of **anonymity** that lowers restraint.
- **Moral relativism** ("right for you, right for me") may be taught in colleges, but it gets people fired at work. People usually know when they are doing wrong.
- **Stakes:** Enron and Andersen show that an ethical failure can destroy an organisation and damage a whole profession.

#### 2. Five related concepts
| Concept | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethics** | Personally accepted principles (also a corporate sense of ethics) | Drives company policy and reputation |
| **Morals** | Accepted from an authority, usually cultural or religious | |
| **Policy** | Corporate framework, from mission statement to top-level policy | Often what "corporate culture" refers to |
| **Law** | State | Overlaps with ethics but differs from it: some laws are unethical (Jim Crow); some are widely ignored without ethical cost. Law works as **deterrence** |
| **Culture** | Norms of a community: corporate, national, global, internet | Can change fast |

#### 3. Formal vs informal ethics, and leadership
- **Formal ethics:** written codes, policies and ethics opinions. Most organisations lack one.
- **Informal ethics:** unwritten but strongly held. **Example:** conference "party" culture disappeared once certification raised standards, and nobody wrote a memo.
- **Gap = cost:** when formal and informal ethics diverge, turnover rises and commitment falls.
- **Ethical leadership** does not come with a title.

#### Key Takeaways for ITW601
1. Activity 1 used this lens: *policy* (paper disposal) vs *ethics* (confidentiality on the reverse side).
2. At my placement, the informal ethic of "just pull the data" vs the formal one ("de-identified, staff-reviewed") is the gap to watch.

### 2. Information Technology Professionals Association. (n.d.). Code of ethics.

**Citation:** Information Technology Professionals Association. (n.d.). *Code of ethics*. https://www.itpa.org.au/code-of-ethics/

**Purpose:** A short, first-person professional code for IT practitioners. Local copy (older wording): [r2_itpa-code-of-ethics.pdf](r2_itpa-code-of-ethics.pdf). Use the current site for current obligations.

---

#### 1. The principles (local copy)
| # | Principle | Core commitment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fair treatment** | No discrimination |
| 2 | **Privacy** | Access private data only when the duties require it; keep it confidential |
| 3 | **Communication** | Keep users informed about acceptable use, monitoring and legal obligations |
| 4 | **System integrity** | Maintain systems and prevent unauthorised access |
| 5 | **Co-operation** | Support fellow professionals and the shared network |
| 6 | **Honesty** | Be honest about my competence, seek help, declare conflicts of interest |
| 7 | **Education** | Keep learning and share knowledge |
| 8 | **Social responsibility** | Understand the social and legal issues and communicate them |
| 9 | **Workplace quality** | A safe, healthy, productive workplace |

#### 2. ACM comparison (extra)

### 3. Global Ethics Solutions. (2020). Ethics in the workplace: What good work ethic really means.

**Citation:** Global Ethics Solutions. (2020). *Ethics in the workplace: What good work ethic really means* [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_n6i1ug0tQ

**Purpose:** A short video on how **work ethic, personal character and ethical behaviour** connect. Transcript: [r3_ethics-in-workplace-transcript.md](r3_ethics-in-workplace-transcript.md).

---

#### 1. Main points
- Work ethic, character and ethical behaviour at work are **interconnected**.
- **Many influences:** culture, generation, parenting, self-discipline, character.
- **Morality is part of ethics:** we dislike having someone else's morality pushed on us, but others' morality shapes our decisions anyway, and "there is no such thing as ethics without morality".
- **Rules vs judgement:** some parts of work are heavily regulated by rules and others are not. Moral judgement makes compliance part of character, not just obedience.

#### Key Takeaways for ITW601
1. This links directly to Activity 1, scenario 3 (personal calls): a fair, consistent standard matters more than a ban.

### 4. Chugh, D. (2018). How to let go of being a "good" person and become a better person.

**Citation:** Chugh, D. (2018, October). *How to let go of being a "good" person - and become a better person* [Video]. TED.

**Purpose:** A social psychologist argues that our attachment to a "good person" identity blocks learning from ethical mistakes. Transcript: [r4_ted-chugh-transcript.md](r4_ted-chugh-transcript.md).

---

#### 1. Bounded rationality → bounded ethicality
- **Bounded rationality:** about 11 million pieces of information come into the mind each moment, and only about 40 are processed consciously, so the brain relies on shortcuts.
- **Bounded ethicality:** the same shortcuts lead "good people" astray in hiring, jokes and business decisions.
  - **Unconscious bias:** associations that don't match conscious beliefs (Nosek, Banaji & Greenwald).
  - **Conflicts of interest:** small gifts quietly bias judgement.

#### 2. The "red zone" and the good-ish person
- A challenge to our moral identity triggers **red-zone defensiveness**, so we explain mistakes away instead of learning from them.
- The binary "good or not" view leaves no room to grow.
- **Good-ish standard:** expect mistakes, look for them, own them, learn from them.

#### Key Takeaways for ITW601
1. In the A3 reflection, owning "I over-claimed the ML scope" is a good-ish move, not a weakness.
2. Conflicts of interest connect to M4 (FNB): a PM who stays silent before his own appraisal.
