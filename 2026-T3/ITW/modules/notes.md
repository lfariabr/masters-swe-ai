## Module 1 - Introduction to WIL

### TLDR
WIL turns theory into practice under real workplace constraints. Module 1 sets up why professional identity matters (Boughton's ICT professional types), what "quality" WIL looks like from five stakeholder perspectives (CEWIL's Triple-A framework: Aims, Actions, Achievements), and starts the career-facing groundwork (introductions, LinkedIn) that feeds Assessment 1. Practical example: this student's own placement — building a production parent-portal dashboard for St Catherine's School — is itself a live case study in balancing student aims (skill growth), host-organisation aims (a working system leadership can evaluate), and educator aims (assessable, reflective learning) at once.

### Introduction
Welcome to ITW601 Information Technology: Work Integrated Learning. Work Integrated Learning is an umbrella term that is often used to encapsulate any kind of learning that has a practical element to it. Therefore, it’s not simply learning about the theory of the subject, it’s also learning how to apply it in the real world. This subject aims not only to develop your theoretical skills but also to enhance your skills in applying the theoretical concepts you learn about to the professional environment.

In the work environment, professional practices are of utmost importance as they are directly related to your success within the organisation. This subject will assist you to establish your professional identity by providing you with an overview of the Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) occupations and the variety of future career paths available to you as an IT professional.

#### 3. Information and Communication Technology Types
- APA Reference: Boughton, C. (2013). What is an ICT professional anyway? In J. Weckert & R. Lucas (Eds.), Professionalism in the information and communication technology industry (pp. 77-94). ANU E Press. https://ebookcentral-proquest-com.torrens.idm.oclc.org/lib/think/reader.action?docID=4585025&ppg=85

After reading the resource “What is an ICT Professional Anyway?” (Boughton, 2013), based on your experience, enlighten the following types of traits mixed with professionalism. Based on your experience, describe an example of each type and give pros and cons of each type.

- The ‘highly broadly technical and you know it’ type
- The ‘highly broadly technical and you don’t know it’ type
- The ‘highly narrowly technical and you know it’ type
- The ‘highly narrowly technical and you don’t know it’ type
- The ‘big-picture broadly technical’ type
- The ‘big-picture narrowly technical’ type
- The ‘concepts only’ type
- The ‘I can do it’ type
- The ‘I can’t do it’ type
- The ‘other professional’ type
Post your responses in the Module 1 discussion forum.

Please read other students’ posts and get yourself familiar with other students in the class as well.

**Posted (class, Module 1; grounded in the Family Hub and Review Pulse projects):**
- **'Highly broadly technical and you know it'**: a senior engineer I consulted while designing Family Hub's read-only BFF boundary - deep, correct opinions on every layer (SQL, auth, caching), freely shared, occasionally at a pace that steamrolls discussion. Pro: the architecture decisions held up under review. Con: junior input got talked over more than once.
- **'Big-picture broadly technical'**: closer to how I'd describe my own role on Family Hub - I couldn't out-detail a database specialist on `mssql` internals, but I could hold the whole system (SSO boundary, data-minimisation rule, PDF export, promotion narrative) in view and sequence the build so each piece stayed defensible. Pro: kept scope aligned to the governance constraints. Con: verification of some low-level SQL query details needed a second pair of eyes.
- **'I can do it'**: myself, early on Review Pulse - confident enough with BiLSTM to skip a proper baseline, until a plain TF-IDF model beat it on held-out data. Pro: the enthusiasm got a full experiment shipped fast. Con: no baseline meant I couldn't prove the fancier model earned its complexity until I went back and added one.
- **'Concepts only'**: a stakeholder conversation about the ML intervention system - someone with a real sense of *what* the school needed (early signal on students needing support) but no view on *how* de-identified data constraints shape what's technically possible. Pro: kept the project anchored to an actual need. Con: the initial ask assumed data access that data-privacy rules would never allow.

### Assessment
#### Recommended Task
This is where this module's learning turns into assessment progress.

Think about the type of ICT professional you would like to become after graduation. Consider the industries, technologies, projects, or roles that interest you most.

In one paragraph, describe your ideal professional role and identify two skills or capabilities you already possess that support this career pathway. Then add one sentence explaining one skill you would like to develop further during your Work Integrated Learning experience.

This activity will help you begin reflecting on your professional identity and career goals, both of which are important foundations for Assessment 1 and your broader Work Integrated Learning experience.

 

#### Assessment Progression
Assessment 1 is due at the end of Module 4. Please refer to the assessment brief in the assessment area for more details.

You are expected to spend approximately one hour working on Assessment 1 in this module. You should:

Review and familiarise yourself with the Assessment 1 brief.
Enhance your understanding about writing effective project proposals and reflective reports.
Understand all the important concepts from this module which will assist you with successfully completing Assessment 1.
 

#### Prepare for Assessment 1
To complete your assessment on time, it is recommended that you complete the following weekly tasks:

Review the Assessment 1 brief and identify the assessment requirements, submission expectations, and marking criteria. Pay particular attention to the requirements relating to project proposals and reflective reporting.

Create a professional development plan by identifying your current ICT knowledge, technical skills, industry experience, and areas for future growth.

Complete Learning Activity 1 and use the introduction process to begin building relationships with peers and learning about different ICT career pathways and professional interests.

Update or create your LinkedIn profile and begin building your professional online presence. Connect with classmates and start developing a professional network that may support your future career.

Review the ACS Core Body of Knowledge (CBOK) and identify the professional attributes, capabilities, and expectations that are relevant to your preferred career pathway.

Develop a draft project idea or professional learning goal that you would like to explore during your placement or industry project. Consider how it relates to your career interests and future aspirations.

Create a study and project schedule that allocates time for learning activities, assessment preparation, workplace commitments, and professional reflection throughout the trimester.

Assessment 1 is due at the end of Module 4.

Submitting early? First, complete this module's activities and check your work against the assessment brief. Successful submissions demonstrate an understanding of professional practice, career development, and how academic learning can be applied in workplace environments.
